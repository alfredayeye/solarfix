The Cape Town School of Human Flight

An inclusive indoor skydiving programme for people with disabilities, and a pathway into FAI-recognised Indoor Para Skydiving. An initiative led by Ruben Knoetze, National and International Springbok Skydiving Champion.

Executive summary

The opportunity to fly.

The Cape Town School of Human Flight (SOHF) gives people with disabilities, particularly those living with spinal cord injuries and paraplegia, amputations, cerebral palsy, autism and visual impairments, the opportunity to experience human flight in a purpose-built vertical wind tunnel.

A wind tunnel produces a controlled column of air that lets a person experience bodyflight indoors, under professional supervision, without needing to jump from an aircraft. For many participants this will be their first experience of an extreme sport considered off-limits to them. For others, it will be the start of a path toward competitive Indoor Para Skydiving.

SOHF is proposed to operate from the planned Cape Town Vertical Wind Tunnel, built around a 6.5-metre diameter TT65 PRO flight chamber. That scale matters directly: a wider, more spacious chamber makes assisted entry, transfer and supported flight materially easier and safer for wheelchair users than the smaller tunnels typical of most facilities.

The programme

Shaped around each participant.

Every participant's programme is shaped around their individual mobility, strength and goals, developed by European-qualified professionals. Participants move through the programme at their own pace, and may stop at any point: a single unforgettable flight, a regular recreational activity, or a serious sporting pursuit.

  1. Experience flight
  2. Build confidence
  3. Learn bodyflight skills
  4. Structured training
  5. Competition
  6. National & international representation

SOHF's role is to open the door, not to prescribe the destination.

Indoor Para Skydiving

The FAI's first para-sports discipline.

Indoor Para Skydiving is formally recognised by the Fédération Aéronautique Internationale (FAI), the world governing body for air sports, which adopted it in 2025 as its first-ever para-sports discipline. A World Cup debuted in France in April 2026, with the inaugural FAI World Championships following in March 2027, under the FAI's International Skydiving Commission.

SOHF intends to work with the Aero Club of South Africa, the country's FAI-recognised National Air Sport Control, alongside paraplegic associations and disability sporting organisations, to identify candidates, provide introductory flights, build structured training pathways, and prepare athletes for FAI-sanctioned competition, with the goal of South African representation at future World Championships.

“If I can do this, what else can I do?”What participants gain: confidence, independence, discipline, teamwork and a genuine sporting pathway.

Facility, safety & accessibility

The physical detail matters as much as the vision.

SOHF is committed to developing, and publishing, the following in partnership with disability organisations, adaptive-sport specialists and rehabilitation professionals:

  • Medical clearance and individual risk assessment criteria for participation
  • A transfer and entry procedure for wheelchair users, including harnessing and assisted-flight technique
  • Instructor certification specific to adaptive and assisted bodyflight
  • Step-free access to the flight deck, changing facilities, bathrooms and spectator areas
  • An on-site emergency and medical response protocol appropriate to the participant group

Impact

What participants gain.

  • Confidence, independence and personal achievement
  • Discipline, goal-setting and concentration
  • Teamwork, communication and social belonging
  • A genuine sporting pathway, from first flight to competitive athlete
  • For young participants: leadership, new friendships and long-term sporting opportunity

An inclusive sporting community

Built with the disability community, not simply for it.

Participants train alongside able-bodied athletes, coaches and instructors where appropriate, and are recognised first as athletes and members of the flying community. This requires a coordinated partnership model.

Our vision

Not as spectators, but as flyers, athletes and competitors.

We want to see people with disabilities entering the Cape Town School of Human Flight as members of the flying community and, ultimately, as South African para-athletes representing their country on the international stage. SOHF is committed to safety, respect, dignity, inclusion, equal opportunity and sporting excellence.
